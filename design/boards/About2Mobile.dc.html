News, measured as waves.

Breaker Delta reads the places where news starts, every hour. When a second independent source picks up the same story, it becomes a wave. Fourteen days later we check whether we were right, in public.

  1. 1. An echo

    One source mentions something: a repository taking off, a forum post, a lab’s blog. We record it and wait.

  2. 2. A wave

    A second independent source picks it up. Echoes on the same story stack, and only then do we write a summary.

  3. 3. A verdict

    The hour a wave forms we lock a test that can fail. Fourteen days later it is checked against outside reporting.

How tall is a wave?

The number beside every headline. It has no unit. It only means something next to the other waves.

EchoesHow many separate items mention the story.
SourcesHow many independent sources they come from.
AttentionPoints, stars and replies on those items, where the source reports them.
FreshnessWith nothing new, the height halves every 48 hours.

A story stays on the front page only while people keep talking about it. After 7 quiet days it leaves; its page stays.

What counts as independent?

Ten outlets repeating one wire story are one voice, not ten. Sources are fetched in groups, and a whole group counts once however many of its outlets carry the story.

Origin. Where stories tend to start: forums, code hosts, lab blogs, key accounts.Pickup. Where they spread: press groups, news search, newsletters.
