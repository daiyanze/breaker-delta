The summary, when it is not a normal one

A reference sheet, not a page readers reach. Board 03 shows “What happened” with a checked, published summary. These are the four other things that section can show.

1. Unverified
The summary names a person or company in a dismissal, lawsuit, accident or death, and fewer than two first-hand sources back it.

What happened

Unverified

We could not confirm this with two first-hand sources. The wording below says only what was reported and by whom.

Two outlets report that the chipmaker’s board has decided not to bring back its founder as chief executive. The company has not commented. Neither report names its source.

This summary was written by a model. Who does whatThe label sits directly under the section name, before the first word of the summary. No drop cap. The summary is left out of every email and the share card shows the headline only. When a second first-hand source arrives, the next version is published without the label.
2. Source text not read
The articles could not be opened, so the model had only the short descriptions that come with the links.

What happened

Source text not read

Six regional banks reported outages on the same afternoon. The descriptions published with the links point to a shared software vendor.

This summary rests on the sources’ own descriptions only; we could not open the articles. This summary was written by a model. Who does whatSame layout as a normal summary, drop cap included. The label and the first sentence of the small line are the only differences. It can appear together with “Unverified”; then both labels show, Unverified first.
3. Withdrawn
The desk withdrew the summary. This happens within a minute of the decision.

What happened

We withdrew this summary on Nov 14 at 10:20. It named the wrong company. A corrected summary will appear here when one is written.

The clippingsThe sources’ own headlines follow here, as on board 03.
The summary text is gone from the page, the feed and the share card; the note stays for good. The clippings, the wave’s height and its history are untouched. Our 14-day call stays and will still be judged. The withdrawal appears in the list of changes.
4. No summary yet
The topic is a wave but no summary has been written.

What happened

No summary yet. This became a wave at 14:00. The summary is written on the next run, at about 15:00.

The clippingsThe sources’ own headlines follow here, as on board 03.

If the site has no summary writer set up, the note reads:

This site does not write summaries. What follows are the sources’ own headlines.

No model-written text anywhere on the page: the headline is the first source’s own headline and there is no call stub. The same note is used when a version was thrown away because it failed its checks.
