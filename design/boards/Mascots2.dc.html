Delta and Breaker

Two mascots, named after the two halves of the product. Delta the cat lives at the newsstand and stands for the people who run it. Breaker the gull lives at sea and carries things between the sea and the reader. Neither one talks.

Delta, the stand cat

Black, with yellow eyes, a pink nose and pink inside the ears. Flat shapes, no outline. Appears on the newsstand roof, and wherever the stand or the desk is resting.
NappingIts usual state. Paws tucked in, eyes shut.
Half awakeAfter one press: eyes narrowed, large pupils, tail wagging.
StandingLegs out. Used when it jumps away and comes back.
WalkingTail straight up, the way a cat greets someone. For first run.
StretchingJust woken. For the first edition of the day.
SittingFacing the reader. For empty states and the desk sign-in.
From behindLooking away, at the sea. For pages that end.
Curled upDeep sleep. For the quiet front page and scheduled downtime.
PeekingOver any edge. For not found and for things still loading.

Breaker, the gull

White, with an ink outline, a pale blue wing, a yellow beak with a red spot, dark wing tips and a pink cheek. Appears in the footer sky, beside the message in a bottle, in the emails and on the subscribe messages.
Far awayTwo arcs. The form already used in the footer and above the bottle.
FlyingArriving or leaving. Unsubscribed.
With a letterDelivering. For subscribe sent and both editions by email.
StandingOn a rock or the lighthouse rail.
FloatingResting on the water beside the bottle.
From the frontLooking at the reader. For greetings and confirmations.
From behindLooking out to sea. For pages that end.
On a postWaiting. For sources that are late and for the next scan.
CallingBeak open. For corrections and for errors that need attention.

Rules: one mascot per illustration. In copy, a name always comes with what it is: Delta the cat, Breaker the gull. Breaker keeps the same colours in both themes: pure white, a dark line, a pale blue wing. Delta is ink-dark on paper; on the dark theme it lightens to slate so it stays visible against the night background, and its eyes stay yellow. They do not wear clothes, speak or show text. All motion stops when reduced motion is on.
